03 Market Context

Demand Is Exploding.
Supply Never Caught Up.

Four years ago, our own research counted 50 companies building motorhomes in Argentina and 70 in Brazil. Today there are 500 and 700. The market didn't just grow, it exploded, especially since the pandemic, and production capacity never caught up.

50→500
Motorhome companies in Argentina, in just four years.
70→700
Motorhome companies in Brazil, in just four years.
85%
Of motorhomes in the region are still built by hand, not on a production line.
Only 2–3 factories in Argentina and 4–5 in Brazil run true serial production, nothing close to European scale. Caravan Salon is marking its 65th year; Expo Motorhome, its 10th. This is a young industry with an old world's worth of catching up to do.

03 Market Context

A Market Waiting
for Real Brands

The Brand Gap

Leading European brands are already present, but distributors often resell them under private labels to protect exclusivity, so end customers rarely know whose product they're actually buying. Chinese vehicles and components are starting to arrive too, but quality concerns and unfamiliarity keep buyers cautious. There's real room, especially in the mid-tier, between artisanal shops and premium imports.

The Sprinter Problem

Buyers still follow the same two-step path: a car dealership for a Mercedes-Benz Sprinter, then a motorhome factory for the conversion. To most of them, Sprinter isn't just a base vehicle, it's the only one that counts, the idealized standard nothing else seems to compare to. That's a perception gap other manufacturers can close.

What the industry needs isn't just more product. It's process, technology, technique and knowledge, homologation and certification. But above all, it needs culture, the industrial culture Europe has spent decades building.
